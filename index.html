How the figures are worked out


When it is late. If you agreed a payment date, the invoice is late from the next day. If you did not, the law says it is late 30 days after the customer gets the invoice, or 30 days after you deliver, if that is later. Agreed terms are usually up to 60 days between businesses and up to 30 days for public authorities.


Interest. Statutory interest is 8% a year plus the Bank of England base rate. The base rate used is the one in force on 30 June or 31 December immediately before the invoice became late, and it stays the same for that debt even if the Bank changes the rate later. Daily interest is the yearly figure divided by 365. This calculator rounds to the penny only at the end, so it can be a few pence higher than the GOV.UK worked example, which rounds the daily figure first.


Fixed sum. Once per invoice you can add £40 for a debt up to £999.99, £70 for £1,000 to £9,999.99, and £100 for £10,000 or more.


When it does not apply. Not for private customers, and not if your contract sets its own interest rate (use that instead).


Figures from the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 as set out on GOV.UK, and the Bank of England Bank Rate history (table last checked ).
